## Abstract (condensed)
Long explicit CoT is effective but costly at inference; non-verbal (continuous) reasoning
methods shorten generation but lag verbalized CoT in performance. Abstract
Chain-of-Thought (Abstract-CoT) is a discrete latent reasoning post-training mechanism:
the model produces a short sequence of tokens from a reserved vocabulary in lieu of a
natural-language CoT before generating the response. A policy-iteration-style warm-up
loop alternates (i) bottlenecking from a verbal CoT via masking + SFT and (ii)
self-distillation (learning to generate abstract tokens from the prompt alone via
constrained decoding with the codebook). After warm-up, the abstract-sequence generation
is optimized with warm-started RL under constrained decoding. Abstract-CoT achieves up to
11.6x fewer reasoning tokens with comparable performance across mathematical
reasoning, instruction-following, and multi-hop reasoning, generalizes across LLM
families, and shows an emergent power-law distribution over the abstract vocabulary akin
to natural language.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Discrete abstract-token latent CoT: a reserved vocabulary replaces natural-language
  rationales; up to 11.6x fewer reasoning tokens at comparable performance.
- Learned via warm-up (masked SFT bottleneck + self-distillation) then warm-started RL
  under constrained decoding; emergent power-law over the abstract vocabulary.

## Relevance to core question
Assessment: discrete-latent-reasoning method (the "compressed non-linguistic tokens"
family of the latent-reasoning landscape). The key finding - a learned abstract
reasoning vocabulary with a power-law distribution - is evidence that efficient latent
intermediates can be trained rather than natively present. It is a post-training
mechanism (SFT + RL), so it is on the elicitation side, not native forward-pass
reasoning. Fails out-of-the-box (trained post-training; standard math/instruction/
multi-hop tasks). Meets none of the five acceptance conditions. Overall verdict
unchanged.
