## Abstract (condensed)
Training-free early-exit methods shorten long, redundant CoT traces by injecting an
end-of-think token (EoT,
</think>) at an intermediate point to trigger the reasoning-to-answering transition.
This paper finds the injected EoT does not always induce a clean answering phase:
answering-phase generation can continue before the model regenerates another EoT, with the
preceding span scaling with the reasoning tokens saved and exhibiting continued
reasoning behavior ("spurious CoT termination"). It hypothesizes insufficient attention
to the injected EoT contributes and probes this with Exit-token Attention Biasing (EAB).
Across four large reasoning models (LRMs), five benchmarks, and two early-exit methods,
increasing attention to the injected EoT reduces spurious CoT termination and
answering-phase length.

## Key findings (from abstract)
- Externally injecting the EoT token to conform to the model's think-block format does not
  by itself guarantee the intended reasoning-to-answering transition (spurious CoT
  termination).
- Attention to the injected EoT is the operative variable: increasing it (EAB) reduces
  spurious termination and answering-phase length (four LRMs, five benchmarks, two
  early-exit methods).
- Reveals a limitation of controlling LRMs by externally matching their explicit
  think-block format.

## Relevance to core question
Assessment: faithfulness/control line (C4-relevant). Directly relevant to the repo's
standing caution (Lanham/Turpin, round 3) that verbalized CoT is not reliable control:
it shows a format-level control signal (the EoT token) is not a faithful or reliable
switch between the reasoning and answering phases. Fails out-of-the-box (it studies
existing reasoning models under an external control intervention). Meets none of the
five acceptance conditions. Overall verdict unchanged.
