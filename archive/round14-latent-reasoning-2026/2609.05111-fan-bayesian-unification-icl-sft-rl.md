## Abstract (condensed)
Treats SFT, few-shot in-context learning (ICL), KL-regularized RLHF/RLVR, and on-policy
distillation as instances of a single two-step template: construct a (generalized) Bayes
or Gibbs posterior from a reference model and a utility signal (log-likelihood, reward,
or advantage), then approximate it by a forward-KL projection onto a parametric family,
either in-weights (SFT/RL) or in-context (ICL). Yields a single chain of equivalences:
few-shot ICL is an amortized projection onto the Bayes posterior predictive;
reward-weighted SFT/ICL and advantage-weighted SFT are forward-KL projections of
reward-induced Gibbs posteriors. Explains why supervised warm-up is practically
unavoidable for importance-weighted projections and interprets R1/o1-style reasoning
models as combining test-time Bayesian search with training-time amortization.
Matched-budget experiments on Qwen3 models corroborate the picture.

## Key findings (from abstract)
- One template unifies SFT / ICL / KL-regularized RL / on-policy distillation as
  Bayes-or-Gibbs posterior construction plus forward-KL projection.
- Predicts operators sharing the same learning-signal granularity produce near-identical
  updates when support is good and diverge when it degrades; reward-weighted projection
  performs on par with standard baselines (Qwen3 matched-budget).

## Relevance to core question
Assessment: theory-line consolidation (the ICL-as-algorithm thread, extending Garg 2022
and Akyurek 2023 in the archive). It formalizes why in-context generalization is an
amortized projection that needs support (demonstrations or warm-up) - a constraint on
the out-of-the-box hope, not evidence for it. Fails the empirical out-of-the-box demand
entirely (no base model, no novel procedural task). Meets none of the five acceptance
conditions. Overall verdict unchanged.
