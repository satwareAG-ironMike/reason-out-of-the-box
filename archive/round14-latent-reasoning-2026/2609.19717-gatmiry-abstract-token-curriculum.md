## Abstract (condensed)
CoT techniques require explicit supervision on thinking tokens, which requires rich
task-specific data. Abstract Token Curriculum (ATC) is a curriculum framework that
elicits effective continuous intermediate representations without direct supervision or
manual scratchpad design: it gradually increases problem complexity through a sequence of
distributions, training the model to develop internal abstract "thoughts" in the
continuous representation space. Provides both theoretical and experimental evidence.
Theoretically, for learning parity functions with single-layer softmax attention under
ATC, attention naturally focuses on the CoT tokens that provide the "easiest path" to
predicting the next token. Experimentally, ATC is effective on graph reachability and
arithmetic learning tasks.

## Key findings (from abstract)
- Curriculum elicits continuous internal "thoughts" without scratchpad supervision.
- Theory: for parity functions under single-layer softmax attention, ATC drives attention
  to the CoT tokens offering the easiest next-token prediction path.
- Experiments on graph reachability and arithmetic.

## Relevance to core question
Assessment: theory-line contribution to "what internal abstract thoughts a transformer
can form and how". The parity-function/single-layer-attention result is a small formal
characterization of when continuous thoughts help. Fails out-of-the-box: ATC is a
training curriculum (post-training), and the tasks (parity, graph reachability,
arithmetic) are the standard algorithmic-learning benchmark, not procedurally generated
post-cutoff tasks with a human baseline. Meets none of the five acceptance conditions.
Overall verdict unchanged.
