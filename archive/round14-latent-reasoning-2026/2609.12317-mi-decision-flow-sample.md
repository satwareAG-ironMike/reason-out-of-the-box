## Abstract (condensed)
Builds on the distribution-sharpening hypothesis (RL reallocates probability mass toward
high-reward trajectories already latent in base models) and asks whether those latent
paths can be unlocked without RL. Decision-Flow Sampling (DF-Sample) is a training-free,
data-free inference-time framework that builds a hierarchical reasoning tree, scores
terminal nodes for quality, and back-propagates utilities to guide each intermediate
branching decision. Unlike local step-wise sampling it performs explicit global
trajectory evaluation before committing, recovering high-quality but low-probability
chains that standard decoding overlooks. On GPQA it reaches 45.6%, above power sampling
(38.9%) and GRPO (39.9%); consistent across three models and four benchmarks.

## Key findings (from abstract)
- Training-free, data-free inference-time extraction of latent reasoning paths; no RL
  fine-tuning.
- GPQA: DF-Sample 45.6% vs power sampling 38.9% and GRPO 39.9% - a training-free method
  outperforms a trained one.
- Consistent wins across three models and four benchmarks, read as evidence of substantial
  latent reasoning potential in pretrained base models.

## Relevance to core question
Assessment: strong H0-b datapoint. Supports the latent-capability line (reasoning paths
already present in base models, extractable training-free). Fails the out-of-the-box
demand because the extraction is an explicit inference-time search scaffold (a tree with
utility back-propagation), not a native forward pass. Meets none of the five acceptance
conditions: the model is pretrained and the tasks are not procedurally generated
post-cutoff. Overall verdict unchanged.
