## Abstract (condensed)
CoT improves reasoning but incurs substantial computation and context cost; existing
methods either lose intermediate information through hard pruning or lack a principled
criterion for continuous compression. A*-Thought-V2 models CoT as a hidden-state
trajectory and replaces hard deletion with an explicit-implicit interleaved latent
architecture. After projecting question, step, and solution representations into a 3D PCA
space, it measures alignment between each local transition and the global
question-to-solution direction: aligned steps stay explicit text, deviating steps are
compressed into continuous latent tokens. Directional angles capture local semantics and
reasoning dynamics (small = direct execution / answer formation; large = checking,
correction, branch exploration). Trained via stepwise embedding forcing (pool each
redundant step into one latent embedding) and label forcing (soft multi-modal
vocabulary distribution instead of a hard one-hot label). On Qwen3.5-9B and
Qwen3.6-27B across six in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks: up to +2.6% average
accuracy, up to half the response length, 2.29x Accuracy per Computation Unit, 94.6%
less preprocessing and up to 80.3% less training time.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Geometric criterion (local-to-global alignment in 3D PCA space) decides which CoT steps
  stay explicit versus compress to latent tokens.
- Up to +2.6% average accuracy with up to 2x shorter responses and 2.29x accuracy per
  computation unit (Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen3.6-27B; six benchmarks).

## Relevance to core question
Assessment: H0-b-adjacent latent-reasoning method. Uses the model's own hidden-state
geometry as the compression criterion, but the interleaved latent architecture is
trained (stepwise embedding forcing + label forcing) - an elicitation scaffold, not a
native forward pass. The geometric-dynamics framing is a useful characterization of
where latent states sit relative to textual states. Fails out-of-the-box (trained
architecture; standard in/out-domain benchmarks). Meets none of the five acceptance
conditions. Overall verdict unchanged.
