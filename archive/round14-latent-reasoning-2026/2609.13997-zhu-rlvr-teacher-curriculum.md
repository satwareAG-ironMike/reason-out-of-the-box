## Abstract (condensed)
RLVR improves mathematical reasoning, but problems beyond the model's current capability
(rollouts uniformly fail, no learning signal) are structurally wasted despite marking
the most informative training frontier. This paper shows these otherwise-inert problems
can be unlocked via teacher-guided curriculum learning: partial reasoning traces from a
stronger model create a graded difficulty landscape, and a backward-chaining curriculum
progressively withdraws guidance until the model solves problems unaided. Training on
only 128 unsolvable problems matches or exceeds GRPO trained on a full 2,000-problem
corpus (~16x data efficiency) on the nine-benchmark average for both base models, while
substantially expanding the reasoning boundary measured by pass@k at large k. Identifies
a distribution-shift cost acute in the unsolvable-only regime and proposes Monotone
Frontier Curriculum (MFC), which monotonically drives training toward unguided solving
and consistently outperforms existing curriculum methods.

## Key findings (from abstract)
- Teacher-guided backward-chaining curriculum unlocks otherwise-inert (unsolvable)
  problems; 128 unsolvable problems match/exceed GRPO on a 2,000-problem corpus (~16x
  data efficiency).
- Substantially expands the reasoning boundary (pass@k at large k); Monotone Frontier
  Curriculum addresses the unsolvable-only distribution-shift cost.

## Relevance to core question
Assessment: RLVR line (post-training). Extends the round-9 RLVR boundary evidence (Yue
et al.) with a data-efficient curriculum and the pass@k boundary metric. It is
reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards on base models - the exact elicitation
mechanism the repo's consensus treats as amplifying rather than creating latent
reasoning. Fails out-of-the-box (RL post-training; standard math benchmarks). Meets none
of the five acceptance conditions. Overall verdict unchanged.
