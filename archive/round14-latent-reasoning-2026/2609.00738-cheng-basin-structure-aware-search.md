## Abstract (condensed)
Inference-time search with LLMs often concentrates on a small set of structurally or
semantically similar trajectories, leaving alternatives underexplored - a failure mode
called "reasoning basin collapse". BASIN is a training-free, structure-aware selection
method that groups reasoning states into basins and penalizes repeated visits to the same
strategy, reallocating search across genuinely distinct paths under a fixed compute
budget. Under matched budgets it improves over Tree of Thoughts (ToT) by up to +22pp on
Game of 24 and +6.7pp on MuSR. A quality-aware variant (QA-BASIN) preserves high-quality
basins when unconditional diversification over-explores. Introduces the redundancy gap
Delta, measuring how differently search concentrates for correct versus incorrect
predictions (standard ToT near Delta~0; BASIN shifts Delta positive).

## Key findings (from abstract)
- Names and fixes "reasoning basin collapse" (search concentrates on similar trajectories).
- Training-free basin-aware selection beats ToT by up to +22pp (Game of 24) and +6.7pp
  (MuSR) under matched compute budgets.

## Relevance to core question
Assessment: inference-time search scaffold (the elicitation side). It is a
training-free selection method, but the gain comes from an external structure-aware
search procedure over the model's trajectories, not from a native forward pass - the
deliberate-control contrast for the latent-reasoning cluster. Its redundancy-gap
diagnostic is a useful measure of whether search is actually diversifying. Fails
out-of-the-box (external search). Meets none of the five acceptance conditions. Overall
verdict unchanged.
