## Abstract (condensed)
RLVR drives CoT reasoning, but its binary outcome reward cannot distinguish among
correct trajectories; existing dense rewards (surface heuristics, process reward models)
either ignore expert solutions already in training corpora or need expensive offline
annotation. Gradient-Aligned Reward (GAR) operates in the policy's own gradient space:
truncated backpropagation through the output projection layer extracts a compact
gradient vector per rollout, and cosine similarity with an expert-anchor gradient yields
a dense, reasoning-aware reward with less than 9% wall-clock overhead. Proves the cosine
admits a multiplicative decomposition into prediction-error and activation-pattern
factors. On Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B, GAR consistently improves over GRPO and other
baselines on competition-level math and transfers to GPQA Diamond and MMLU-Pro without
domain-specific data.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Dense, reasoning-aware reward from gradient-space cosine similarity (expert-anchor
  alignment) at less than 9% wall-clock overhead.
- Formal decomposition: the cosine reward factors into prediction-error x
  activation-pattern terms.
- Improves over GRPO on Qwen3-4B/8B for competition-level math; transfers to GPQA
  Diamond and MMLU-Pro without domain-specific data.

## Relevance to core question
Assessment: RLVR line (post-training). A reward-design contribution to RLVR that uses the
policy's own gradients rather than outcome-only or separately-trained process rewards.
Like the rest of the RLVR cluster it is an elicitation mechanism applied during
post-training; it says nothing about whether a base model reasons natively. Fails
out-of-the-box (RL; standard math/GPQA/MMLU-Pro benchmarks). Meets none of the five
acceptance conditions. Overall verdict unchanged.
