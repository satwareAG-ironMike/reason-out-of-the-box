## Abstract (condensed)
Uses the C-RASP / C*-RASP length-generalization frameworks to ask not whether transformers
with CoT *can express* Turing-complete computation, but whether they can *learn* to
generalize CoT traces longer than those seen in training.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- **Result 1**: "under standard positional encodings and a finite alphabet, Transformers
  even with CoT will likely length-generalize only on problems within TC0" - any task
  expressible in C-RASP is definable in TC0. Authors call this "a surprising discovery"
  because prior work attributed CoT's success to transcending TC0: "true for expressivity,
  not true for the stricter requirement of length generalization."
- **Result 2 (the remedy)**: with an infinite alphabet of *signpost* tokens (unique token
  per tape position) and logging only value changes, transformers can length-generalizably
  simulate any Turing machine with CoT length linear in runtime. This generalizes
  "index hints" and explains theoretically why they help.
- Two identified obstacles: repeated copying and last-occurrence retrieval.

## Relevance to core question
**The sharpest 2026 theory barrier, now verified**: trained normally, models cannot reason
at depths beyond their training traces - the formal core of "trained on examples and
hoping it generalizes." The remedy is architectural/representational, not more data:
pointing at exactly the kind of inductive bias biology ships with.
