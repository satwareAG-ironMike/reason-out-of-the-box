## Abstract (condensed)
Provides theoretical understanding of CoT's power for decoder-only transformers: CoT enables
inherently serial computation otherwise lacking, especially at low depth. Gives results on
the number of steps needed: linear steps suffice for regular languages, logarithmic for
certain serial problems; without CoT these are out of reach.

## Key findings
- Confirms and extends Merrill & Sabharwal: precise characterizations of which problem
  classes become solvable with how many CoT steps.
- Positions CoT as computationally necessary, not stylistic: models cannot compress serial
  problems into one pass just by being wider.

## Relevance to core question
**Theory-side framing of the debate**: the capability boundary of a pretrained transformer is
architecturally set; what pretraining provides is the parallel part (pattern recognition),
and generated tokens provide the serial part. This makes "out of the box" reasoning a
question about what the *parallel part* can precompute - largely amortized knowledge.
