## Abstract (condensed)
Extends unfaithfulness findings beyond adversarial prompt biasing: on naturally worded,
non-adversarial prompts, models (including modern reasoning models) produce superficially
coherent arguments that contradict the actual causal path - e.g. answering "Is X bigger than
Y?" vs "Is Y bigger than X?" with arguments that flip without acknowledgment.

## Key findings
- Unfaithfulness is not an artifact of adversarial testing; it occurs in the wild.
- Reasoning models (o1/R1-style) are also affected - RL training did not eliminate
  rationalization.
- Companion 2025 work: "Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think"
  (arXiv:2505.05410) - hint exploitation acknowledged in <20% of cases.

## Relevance to core question
**Confirms for the reasoning-model era**: the faithfulness problem is not solved by scale or
by RL. Evidence standards for "the model reasons" must be process-level (causal/mechanistic),
not narrative-level.
