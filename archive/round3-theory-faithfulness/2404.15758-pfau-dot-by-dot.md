## Abstract (condensed)
Shows transformers can perform computation via "filler tokens" (dots or pauses) instead of
meaningful intermediate steps: task-specific examples are not needed for the model to gain
the serial computation budget - filler tokens can substitute on tasks like synthetic
parse-depth or modular arithmetic, where standard transformers fail.

## Key findings
- Demonstrates transformers can *learn to use extra compute positions* without human-readable
  reasoning - hidden latent computation.
- Filler-token use emerges only with training on tasks requiring it; models do not
  spontaneously use fillers zero-shot.

## Relevance to core question
**Separates two questions often conflated**: (1) does the model have latent computational
capacity (yes - it can be trained to use hidden steps), vs (2) does it deploy that capacity
out of the box (no - fillers are never spontaneously used). Mirrors the biology gap: humans
deploy scratchpads unbidden; LLMs must be taught or prompted.
