## Abstract (condensed)
Studies generalization on small algorithmically generated datasets. Shows networks learn by
"grokking" a pattern: generalization jumps from random chance to perfect long after training
accuracy has saturated (overfitting). Studies dependence on dataset size and weight decay.

## Key findings
- Existence proof that gradient descent **discovers and implements genuine algorithms**
  without being shown them (later mechanistically confirmed by Nanda et al. as Fourier
  arithmetic circuits, see round 4).
- The transition is delayed generalization, not gradual - networks first memorize, then
  reorganize into compact generalizing circuits.
- Follow-ups: Omnigrok (arXiv:2210.01117, LU mechanism), "Deep Networks Always Grok"
  (arXiv:2402.15555) show it is widespread, not a curiosity.

## Relevance to core question
**Supports the "algorithms emerge without explicit examples" half of the claim.** Caveat:
grokking happens *during training* on the task distribution - it shows training can produce
algorithms, not that a pretrained LLM reasons out of the box. Its value is as mechanistic
evidence that transformers are capable of implementing real computation, not mere lookup.
