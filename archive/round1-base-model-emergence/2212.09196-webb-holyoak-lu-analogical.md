## Abstract (condensed)
Direct comparison between GPT-3 (text-davinci-003) and human reasoners on analogical tasks:
novel text-based Digit Matrices (emulating Raven's Progressive Matrices), Hofstadter letter
string analogies, UCLA Verbal Analogy Test, and story analogies - all zero-shot, no training.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- **GPT-3 surpassed UCLA undergraduates** on zero-shot Digit Matrices, generative answers
  (odds ratio 1.88, p=0.005, N=40 humans) and multiple choice (OR 6.27, p=2.3e-8).
- GPT-3's error pattern correlated with the human difficulty profile (r(30)=0.39, p=0.027):
  harder rules were harder for both species - the signature of a shared underlying process,
  not random memorized answers.
- Problems were **structurally isomorphic to Raven's SPM but newly text-ified** - a deliberate
  attempt to avoid visual/memorized solutions. Best human still beat GPT-3.
- Published in a top peer-reviewed cognitive science journal with a direct human baseline -
  the strongest "biological comparison" design in the literature.

## Relevance to core question
**Strongest single peer-reviewed claim of out-of-the-box reasoning.** Novel task family,
zero-shot, human comparison, journal peer review. Remaining gaps: text-only re-rendering of
Raven's may still be near known internet task formats; GPT-3's pretraining exposure to matrix
puzzles is unknowable; single model; no mechanistic account. Critics (Moskvichev-style
sampling arguments) note text-matrices are solvable by learned rule-pattern associations.
