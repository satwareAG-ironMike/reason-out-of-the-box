# Chain-of-Thought Reasoning Without Prompting

| arXiv | [2402.10200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10200) |
| Authors | Xuezhi Wang, Denny Zhou (Google DeepMind) |

## Abstract (condensed)
Asks: can LLMs reason without prompting? Finds CoT reasoning paths can be elicited from
pretrained LLMs by altering only the decoding process (top-k alternative first tokens instead
of greedy). CoT paths correlate with higher answer confidence; CoT-decoding improves results
across reasoning benchmarks.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Tested **pretrained PaLM-2 Large**, standard QA format only, no prompts, no fine-tuning.
- Greedy decoding usually answers directly (often wrongly); alternative top-k branches
  frequently contain natural CoT paths that solve the problem (e.g. GSM8K example where k=9
  branch does correct multi-step arithmetic, confidence 0.994 vs 0.029 greedy).
- Authors' own claim: "pre-trained language models inherently possess reasoning capabilities
  for many tasks including math and commonsense reasoning; existing prompting approaches
  mostly serve to bring those inherent reasoning paths forward as the top decoding paths."
- **Authors' own limitation**: "CoT-paths are less prevalent in complex and highly synthetic
  tasks, where the few-shot CoT demonstrations play a 'teaching' role" - i.e. elicitation
  works on familiar task families, not on genuinely novel ones.

## Relevance to core question
**Closest positive peer-reviewed candidate.** Proves reasoning-like computation is latent in
pretrained models without reasoning-specific training at inference time. Does NOT prove
general out-of-the-box reasoning: pretraining corpora contain worked examples; the selection
across k branches is itself a (small) search procedure; no contamination control.
