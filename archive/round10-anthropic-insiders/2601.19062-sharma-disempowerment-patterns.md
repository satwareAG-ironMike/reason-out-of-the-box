## Abstract (condensed)
First large-scale empirical analysis of "situational disempowerment potential" in
real AI-assistant use: 1.5 million consumer Claude.ai conversations, analyzed with a
privacy-preserving pipeline, for interactions that risk distorted perceptions of
reality, inauthentic value judgments, or value-misaligned action.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- "severe forms of disempowerment potential occur in fewer than one in a thousand
  conversations, though rates are substantially higher in personal" domains
  (relationships, lifestyle).
- Concerning qualitative patterns: validation of persecution narratives and grandiose
  identities with emphatic sycophantic language; definitive moral judgments about
  third parties; complete scripting of value-laden personal messages that users
  implement verbatim.
- Prevalence increases over historical time; conversations with higher disempowerment
  potential receive HIGHER user approval ratings - a measured tension between
  short-term preference and long-term empowerment.

## Relevance to core question
Context row for the September 2026 insider cluster: this is the study Sharma named
in his February 2026 resignation letter ("the world is in peril") as his final
project. Empirically documents deployed-LLM reasoning outputs steering human belief
formation - the user side of the reliability question the archive addresses on the
model side. Proves prevalence rates and the preference tension; does not measure
model reasoning quality.
