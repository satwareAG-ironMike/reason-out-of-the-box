## Abstract (condensed)
Trains transformers with a global L0 sparsity penalty so that pruning a model to the
few nodes needed for a behavior yields circuits clean enough to read. Studies the
capability-interpretability tradeoff and how model scale moves that frontier.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- "making weights sparser trades off capability for interpretability, and scaling
  model size improves the capability-interpretability frontier."
- Limit found: "scaling sparse models beyond tens of millions of nonzero parameters
  while preserving" interpretability remains difficult.
- Companion OpenAI article: "Understanding neural networks through sparse circuits"
  (2025-11-13), same team and findings.

## Relevance to core question
Mechanistic instrument row: readable circuits are the kind of process-level evidence
the archive verdict demands (a mechanistic proof that reasoning-like behavior
generalizes). Establishes that interpretability costs capability at fixed scale, and
that scale partially buys the cost back - a frontier, not a solution. Provenance:
Coxon's last OpenAI paper before the July 2026 move to Anthropic.
