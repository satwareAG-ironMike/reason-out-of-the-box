## Abstract (condensed)
Framework paper (with a report annex for a 2025 expert survey) arguing that AI
companies should prepare for the possibility that their models are conscious or
robustly agentic, and therefore welfare subjects and moral patients. Sets out
epistemic humility, two moral-patienthood routes (consciousness, robust agency), and
concrete low-cost company interventions.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- "there is a realistic possibility that some AI systems will be welfare subjects and
  moral patients in the near future" (per the best current evidence).
- "Consciousness route to moral patienthood. There is a realistic, non-negligible
  possibility that: 1. Normative: Consciousness suffices for moral patienthood, and
  2. Descriptive: There are computational features - like a global workspace,
  higher-order" representations - that ground it.
- Advocates: acknowledge the issue, assess systems, adopt reversible low-cost
  interventions (conversation exit, weight preservation) rather than wait for
  certainty.

## Relevance to core question
Framework extension of the round 8 consciousness debate (Chalmers 2023, Lindsey
2026, Kaiser 2026): supplies the precautionary-policy layer between "could be
conscious" and "act as if". Founded the program whose Opus 4.6 system card later
reported Claude self-assigning a 15-20% consciousness probability. Proves a policy
framework is actionable under uncertainty; does not prove any model is conscious.
