## Abstract (condensed)
The computational theory of cognition needs a theory of implementation: what it is for a
physical system to implement a computation. Chalmers' answer: a system implements a
computation when its causal structure mirrors the formal structure of the computation.
On this foundation, computational sufficiency (the right organization suffices for a
mind) and computational explanation become defensible; mentality is an organizational
invariant, so the substrate performing the organization is not what carries it.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Implementation analysis: "a system implements a computation if the causal structure of
  the system mirrors the formal structure of the computation."
- The two theses: "computational sufficiency, which holds that the right kind of
  computational structure suffices for the possession of a mind", and "computational
  explanation, which holds that computation provides a general framework for the
  explanation of cognitive processes."
- The substrate-independence result: "mentality is an organizational invariant, rooted
  in such patterns."
- Direct answer to Searle: "Along the way I answer various challenges to the
  computationalist position, such as those put forward by Searle"; closing position is a
  "minimal computationalism" compatible with a wide variety of empirical approaches.

## Relevance to core question
**Framework (pro-computationalism); no bearing on the reasoning question.** This paper
states, in its strongest form, the premise the popular hand-execution argument must deny:
if mentality supervenes on causal organization, then which material runs the
organization - silicon, neurons, or a person with paper - does not by itself decide what
is instantiated. The "obvious" non-sentience of a paper execution is therefore not a
neutral data point but the disputed assumption restated. Together with Searle 1980 and
Dennett 1980 this entry documents the full 45-year structure of that dispute; it
establishes nothing about out-of-the-box reasoning, and the overall verdict is unchanged.
