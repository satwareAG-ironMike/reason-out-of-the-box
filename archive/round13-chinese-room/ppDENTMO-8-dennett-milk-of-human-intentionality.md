## Abstract (condensed)
Dennett's BBS commentary on Searle: the Chinese Room is rhetorically effective but
argumentatively empty. It concedes the strawman ("bedridden" programs with no
language-entry or language-exit that serious AI work had already abandoned), and the
remaining force comes from staged intuitions, not from a demonstrated impossibility.
Introduces the term "intuition pump" for this argumentative device.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- The naming of the device: Searle "has constructed what one might call an intuition
  pump, a device for provoking a family of intuitions by producing variations on a basic
  thought experiment. An intuition pump is not, typically, an engine of discovery, but a
  persuader or pedagogical tool."
- The verdict on the argument's force: "I think Searle relies almost entirely on
  ill-gotten gains: favorable intuitions generated by misleadingly presented thought
  experiments."
- The strawman diagnosis: Searle's initial room handles only "bedridden programs" whose
  "only modes of perception and action are linguistic"; "such programs exhibit no
  'language-entry' and 'language-exit' transitions", a limitation "widely recognized for
  years in AI", so "many if not all supporters of strong AI would simply agree with
  Searle" that nobody in the initial room understands Chinese - conceding what the
  argument needs to earn.

## Relevance to core question
**Methodological frame; no bearing on the reasoning question.** This commentary is the
canonical diagnosis of why intuition arguments - including today's popular "if I did the
calculations by hand on paper, would you call the paper sentient?" form - carry no
evidential weight by themselves: they are persuaders, not engines of discovery. It is
the reason the repository keeps conceptual intuition arguments out of the evidence
column (round 8's consciousness balance rests on measured results, not on the room).
Dennett establishes nothing about out-of-the-box reasoning; the overall verdict is
unchanged.
