## Abstract (condensed)
Asks whether LLMs can introspect on their internal states. Because conversation cannot
separate introspection from confabulation, the study **injects representations of known
concepts into a model's activations** and measures whether the model's self-reports track
the manipulation.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- **Claude Opus 4 and 4.1** notice and correctly identify injected concepts "about 20% of
  the time when concepts are injected in the appropriate layer and with the appropriate
  strength."
- Caveat in the authors' words: "aside from the basic detection of and identification of
  the injected concept, the rest of the model's response in these examples may still be
  confabulated"; models "often provide additional details about their purported
  experiences whose accuracy we cannot verify."
- Conclusion: confabulations "do not preclude the possibility that AI models can, at times,
  genuinely introspect, even if they do not always do so."

## Relevance to core question
**The most direct 2026 frontier-model evidence bearing on Hinton's "subjective experience"
claim.** It supports a narrow, causally verified form of functional introspection (20%,
strongest models only) and explicitly refuses the leap to experience. Also the exact test
Lemoine lacked: self-reports checked against manipulated internals rather than taken at
face value.
