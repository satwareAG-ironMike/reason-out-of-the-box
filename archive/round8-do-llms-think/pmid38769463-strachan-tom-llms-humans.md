## Abstract (condensed)
Tests GPT-4, GPT-3.5 and LLaMA2 against 1,907 human participants on a battery of theory
of mind tasks: false belief, irony, faux pas, hinting, strange stories. Compares
distributions of performance, not single scores.

## Key findings
- GPT-4 matched or exceeded human performance on false belief, irony, hinting and
  strange-stories tasks; LLaMA2 lagged.
- GPT-4 **failed the faux pas test** - but follow-up analysis attributed this to
  hyperconservative hesitation to commit to a conclusion (it recognizes the faux pas
  but declines to assert intent), not to a lack of the inference itself.
- LLaMA2 "passed" faux pas for the wrong reason (bias toward attributing ignorance).

## Relevance to core question
**Pro (functional) with a methodological lesson**: peer-reviewed evidence that frontier
models of 2023 produce human-level mentalizing behavior on standardized tests; the faux
pas result shows that failures and successes both need mechanism-level explanation before
they say anything about "thinking."
