## Abstract (condensed)
From the author of the symbol grounding problem (1990): ChatGPT's performance has driven
some to conclude it understands. "It is not true that it understands. But it is also not
true that we understand how it can do what it can do." Argues the capability arises from
biases inherent in language itself at LLM scale (propositional structure, the "mirroring"
of human meaning in text), not from grounded understanding.

## Key findings
- Restates the symbol grounding position for the LLM era: meaning requires sensorimotor
  grounding of at least a minimal set of category names; text-only training cannot
  supply it - a direct theoretical basis for the project's embodiment hypothesis.
- Simultaneously concedes the explanatory gap: the anti-understanding camp cannot yet
  explain the competence either.

## Relevance to core question
**Contra (theoretical), and the strongest scholarly argument for embodiment**: if Harnad is
right, text-only LLMs cannot understand by construction, and the round 7 direction
(sensors, action, a herd) is not an enhancement but a precondition.
