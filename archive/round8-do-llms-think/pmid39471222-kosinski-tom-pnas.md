## Abstract (condensed)
Eleven LLMs assessed on 40 bespoke false-belief tasks (gold standard for theory of mind in
humans). Older models solved none; GPT-3-davinci-003 (Nov 2022) and ChatGPT-3.5 solved 20%
(comparable to 3-year-olds); GPT-4 (June 2023) solved 75% (comparable to 6-year-olds).

## Key findings
- The most cited "LLMs have theory of mind" result, now peer-reviewed after the 2023
  preprint controversy.
- Kosinski's framing: ToM-like ability may have emerged as a by-product of language
  training, not as a designed capability.
- Round 3 counter-evidence remains: Ullman (2023) and Shapira et al. (2023) showed trivial
  perturbations collapse performance on earlier models; the PNAS version uses bespoke
  tasks to reduce contamination.

## Relevance to core question
**Pro (behavioral)**: the peer-reviewed anchor for the claim that mentalizing behavior
scales with model capability. Does not address whether the behavior is understanding
or learned pattern.
