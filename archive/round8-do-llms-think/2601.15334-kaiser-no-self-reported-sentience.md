## Abstract (condensed)
Whether models are sentient has no empirical answer; whether they *believe* they are can be
tested. Queries open-weight models (Qwen, Llama, GPT-OSS; 0.6B to 70B) with ~50 questions
about consciousness and verifies the answers with three classifiers trained on internal
activations (interpretability-literature methods).

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- "The model outputs we observe tend to imply denials of sentience, and classifiers trained
  to detect models' underlying beliefs indicate that these denials are made in earnest."
- "These findings are robust across model families and scales" (0.6B-70B).
- Title's "small" is deliberate: frontier closed models were not tested; the authors bound
  their claim to open weights up to 70B.

## Relevance to core question
**Contra (for Lemoine-style claims)**: the LaMDA transcripts took first-person sentience
statements at face value. Here, when self-reports are checked against internal belief
representations, models deny sentience and mean it. A methodology, not just a verdict -
and one that could be run on frontier models.
