## Abstract (condensed)
Systematic review of the theory-of-mind-in-LLMs literature. Finding: LLMs, particularly
GPT-4, perform well on first-order false-belief tasks but struggle with second-order
beliefs and recursive inferences, where humans consistently outperform them.

## Key findings
- Consolidates the pattern across studies: strong first-order, weak higher-order and
  perturbation robustness - "illusion of understanding" as the review's diagnosis.
- Direct tension with Street et al. (2025, this round), who report adult-level 6th-order
  performance on a new benchmark: the discrepancy tracks benchmark construction and model
  generation, which is itself the finding.

## Relevance to core question
**Contra (systematic)**: peer-reviewed synthesis that behavioral ToM success is shallow
and brittle in the aggregate literature. Read together with Street et al., the honest
summary is: capability is real, robust, and generation-dependent - and none of it settles
experience.
