## Abstract (condensed)
Against computational functionalism: symbolic computation is not an intrinsic physical
process but a "mapmaker-dependent description" that requires an active, experiencing
cognitive agent to carve continuous physics into a finite symbol set. Simulation
(behavioral mimicry, vehicle causality) and instantiation (intrinsic physical
constitution, content causality) are ontologically separate; no scaling of syntactic
machinery crosses the gap. The argument claims to be physicalist (causal closure), not
biological-exclusivist.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Alphabetization vs discretization: thermodynamics yields stable macroscopic
  attractors, never a "predefined finite alphabet"; assigning semantics is an act of
  the mapmaker: "There is no ghost reading the alphabet; the living experiencing
  subject enacts it" (sec 2.3).
- Mechanism indeterminacy ("Melody Paradox", sec 3.3): one physical trajectory supports
  a melody forward, the same melody reversed, stock prices, or noise depending on the
  alphabetization key; computation does not supervene on the substrate.
- Simulation vs instantiation: a GPU simulating photosynthesis "will not synthesize a
  single molecule of glucose or release oxygen"; under causal closure (Kim), experience
  must be physically constituted, so content causality cannot ride on vehicle causality
  (sec 2.5).
- Embodiment concession (sec 4.1): "We agree that an embodied agent does solve the
  referential aspect of the symbol grounding problem (Harnad, 1990)" - but referential
  mapping is not intrinsic sense-making (the "transduction fallacy"); a weather model
  connected to live sensors "does not become the atmosphere".
- Not biological exclusivity: "If an artificial system were ever conscious, it would be
  because of its specific physical constitution, never its syntactic architecture"
  (sec 4.2).
- AI-safety implication (sec 4.2): AGI development refines a "highly sophisticated,
  non-sentient tool"; future sentience claims require physicalist verification of
  constitution, not behavioral mimicry or algorithmic complexity.

## Relevance to core question
**Contra (theoretical) on machine experience; no bearing on the reasoning question.**
The paper is a conceptual analysis: it runs no experiment and measures nothing; its
target is computational functionalism about consciousness, extending the round 8 line
from Harnad (grounding) and Chalmers (framework) to an ontology-of-computation
argument. It sharpens round 8's working distinction between functional competence
(rounds 1 and 4 evidence) and phenomenal experience, which is consistent with the
project verdict that behavioral reasoning evidence does not establish experience.
Caveats for weight: preprint on an unmoderated server, single author, and the 2026
response cluster (7+ PhilPapers-listed manuscripts, several with likely AI-generated
author names) is likewise entirely non-peer-reviewed.
