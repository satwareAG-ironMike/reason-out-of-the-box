## Abstract (condensed)
Assesses how key NLP papers define "reasoning" and argues the definitions are inconsistent
with how LMs are trained and generate. Models a transformer LM as an implicit finite-order
Markov kernel mapping contexts to token distributions; reasoning-like outputs are
statistical regularities of that kernel.

## Key findings
- A definitional critique: the field uses "reasoning" for behaviors its own training
  objective does not license; the mismatch, not the behavior, is the problem.
- Compatible with the round 1-4 picture (amortized pattern completion plus per-token
  compute) and with Mitchell and Krakauer's call for new vocabulary.

## Relevance to core question
**Contra (conceptual)**: even if frontier models produce what looks like thinking, calling
it "reasoning" imports assumptions the mechanism does not meet. Useful as a guard against
both Hinton-style and Lemoine-style overreach - and against under-reach, since the paper
also cannot say what the regularities are *not*.
