## Abstract (condensed)
Argues that mechanistic interpretability findings make the "mere imitation" picture
increasingly untenable - but only once integrated into a theory of understanding. Proposes
a three-tier framework (conceptual, state-of-the-world, principled understanding) and maps
existing interpretability results onto it.

## Key findings
- Turns the archive's round 4 evidence (induction heads, grokked algorithms, attribution
  graphs) into an argument about understanding: features and circuits that track world
  structure are indicators of understanding in a graded, non-anthropocentric sense.
- Explicitly separates understanding from consciousness: the framework claims the former
  and stays silent on experience.

## Relevance to core question
**Pro (mechanistic, philosophical)**: the most careful version of Hinton's "they really do
understand" - grounded in circuits rather than conversation, and stopping short of
subjective experience. The natural counterpart to Harnad in this round.
