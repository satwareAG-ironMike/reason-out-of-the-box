## Abstract (condensed)
Identifies "induction heads": attention-head circuits implementing a [A][B] ... [A] -> [B]
match-and-copy algorithm. Presents causal evidence that these circuits drive much of
in-context learning, and that their formation during training correlates with visible
capability phase changes.

## Key findings
- Real, causally verified algorithms form **spontaneously during ordinary pretraining** -
  no one engineered or exemplified the match-and-copy procedure.
- Induction heads explain a substantial share of few-shot learning ability; their formation
  produces measurable capability jumps (a mechanistic account of one "emergent" ability).

## Relevance to core question
**Supports "pretraining installs general mechanisms without explicit examples."** Counterpoint
kept: matching/copying is a shallow statistical-association mechanism; it is a far cry from
compositional reasoning. It shows the training process finds algorithms, not that the found
algorithms amount to reasoning.
