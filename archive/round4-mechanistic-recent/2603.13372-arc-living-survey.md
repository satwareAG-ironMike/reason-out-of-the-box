## Abstract (condensed)
First cross-generation analysis of 82 approaches across ARC-AGI-1/2/3 and the ARC Prize
2024-2025 competitions. Central finding: performance degradation across versions is
consistent across all paradigms - program synthesis, neuro-symbolic, and neural systems all
drop 2-3x from ARC-AGI-1 to ARC-AGI-2, indicating fundamental limitations in compositional
generalization. Systems now reach 93.0% on ARC-AGI-1 (Opus 4.6).

## Key findings
- No paradigm has escaped the version-to-version drop: whatever solves ARC-AGI-1 does not
  transfer cleanly to structurally more demanding ARC-AGI-2.
- Documents the shift of 2024-2025 progress toward test-time adaptation and program
  synthesis hybrid systems rather than monolithic forward-pass reasoning.

## Relevance to core question
**The 2026 state-of-the-art summary for novel-task reasoning.** Supports a cautious reading:
high benchmark scores coexist with paradigm-level brittleness under task regeneration -
exactly the signature of sophisticated but distribution-bound generalization, not
human-style fluid reasoning.
