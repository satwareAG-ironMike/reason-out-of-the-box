## Summary

Vallor argues that AI systems mirror human values and behaviour back to us, and that
this mirroring creates a false intimacy that invites us to treat artificial systems as
if they were persons. The anchor's first reason cites the book's claim that "baked-in
psychological tendencies" make the conflation of intelligence with consciousness
attractive.

## Relevance to the core question

Supports the anchor's psychological claim about why consciousness attribution to AI
is tempting, and supports the broader claim that our intuitions are a poor guide.
This is the first reason's psychological context rather than a capability boundary.

## Limitations and counterarguments

The argument is about human attribution error, not about AI systems, so it weakens our
confidence in any verdict about consciousness, including sceptical ones.
