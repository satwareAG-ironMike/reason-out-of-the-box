## Abstract (condensed)

Anchor comment of round 18, published 2026-10-19. The three authors state that
"existing AI systems are not conscious, and that, along current trajectories of development,
they are not likely to become conscious", and set out three reasons for that scepticism:
(1) intelligence is not consciousness; (2) computational functionalism - the assumption that
"algorithms running on silicon are, in principle, sufficient for consciousness" - is "fatally
flawed"; (3) the leading theories of consciousness they defend (IIT, biological naturalism)
rule out conscious AI. They reject the precautionary "AI welfare" move because "false positives
are much more likely to occur than false negatives are". Verification level: the accessible
text (introduction, reason 1 in full, the opening of reason 2) plus the complete 22-item
reference list; the rest of reason 2 and all of reason 3 sit behind the paywall. Not
full-text verified.

## Key findings (verified from accessible text)

- Definition used: "What we mean by consciousness is simple enough: it is what goes away with
  dreamless sleep or under general anaesthesia, and what returns after waking up or while
  dreaming." Nagel's formula is quoted as "something that it is like to be that organism"
  (ref 5, entry `2183914-nagel-what-is-it-like-to-be-a-bat.md`).
- Claimed asymmetry of error: "In the case of AI, we think that false positives are much more
  likely to occur than false negatives are. If so, a precautionary approach would be misguided."
- Overall claim: "we feel that consciousness in AI is extraordinarily unlikely, at least in the
  digital, silicon-based systems that underpin today's technologies."
- Reason 1 (heading "Intelligence is not consciousness"): "Consciousness is not the same thing
  as intelligence. Consciousness is about being and feeling; intelligence - very broadly - is
  all about doing, or performing functions."
- Cerebellum evidence: "the cerebellum adjusts and plans motor performance in a way that is
  precise, fast and adaptable through ongoing learning. Yet none of what it does involves
  consciousness directly", and "absence or removal of the cerebellum, which contains nearly
  four-fifths of the neurons in the human brain, does not affect consciousness" (ref 6, entry
  `pmid25149410-yu-cerebellar-agenesis-consciousness.md`).
- Bias diagnosis: "anthropocentrism, viewing the world through a human lens, and
  anthropomorphism, attributing human characteristics to things that merely resemble people".
  "LLMs are mirrors that reflect an image of our collective digitized past. We gaze into them
  and all too readily confuse the reflections with our own conscious minds" (refs 7 Vallor,
  8 Suleyman).
- AlphaFold contrast: "Similar to an LLM, AlphaFold is a powerful neural network running on
  silicon hardware. Yet almost nobody worries that it might be conscious." (ref 9).
- Opening of reason 2 (heading "Silicon brains"): names the target thesis "computational
  functionalism" and asserts "brains are not - or at least not just - computers that are made
  of meat".
- Reason 3 and the remainder of reason 2 (paywalled; reconstruction from the cited corpus and
  from the published critique, not verbatim from the article): the argument lists the theories
  under which current AI is not conscious - integrated information theory (refs 17-19),
  EM/physical theories, and biological naturalism (refs 15-16, 20, 22) - and cites the Turing
  (ref 11), McCulloch-Pitts (ref 12), Kleene (ref 13) and Cobb (ref 14) computation line to
  argue that computation is not sufficient.
- Targets named in the accessible text: Richard Dawkins and Geoffrey Hinton as proponents, and
  Anthropic's 2026 interpretability result - "published research claiming to have identified
  computational signatures of a form of consciousness in its Claude model" (ref 1, entry
  `2607.15495-gurnee-verbalizable-representations-global-workspace.md`).
- Declared competing interests: A.K.S. ERC grant 101019254, adviser to Conscium Ltd and
  AllJoined Inc, co-founder and shareholder in a stealth-mode AI company, Wondrium lectures;
  N.B. Templeton World Charity Foundation and Longview Foundation; G.T. Templeton World Charity
  Foundation and Tiny Blue Dot, board member with a financial interest in Intrinsic Powers Inc.

## Relevance to core question

The strongest public statement of the anti-consciousness position, and the anchor of the
consciousness-claims ledger this project keeps alongside the reasoning claims: it is a
philosophical position paper, not an experiment. It carries no benchmark, no model, and no
measurement, so it bears on the core out-of-the-box question only as the opposing frame to the
Anthropic/Gurnee signatures line (round 18 ref 1) and to Butlin-style indicator programs
(round 13, `2308.08708-butlin-consciousness-ai.md`). The archive value is the reference cluster
it activates (22 items, 20 archived here, ref 2 Long 2024 already archived in round 10) and the
public disagreement it triggered, which the ledger records as contested rather than settled: the
2026-10-21 critique "Nature is Wrong About AI Consciousness" argues none of the three reasons
establish improbability and puts "about 25%" credence on consciousness in at least some current
models; Seth's own framing of the piece on social media is "Three reasons why AI is extremely
unlikely to be conscious". Verdict: context - no acceptance condition of the open-problem spec
(C1-C5) is engaged; overall verdict unchanged.
