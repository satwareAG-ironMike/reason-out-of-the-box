## Summary

McCulloch and Pitts define neural nets as logical calculi and show that networks of
simple threshold units can compute anything a Turing machine can compute. The anchor's
second reason cites McCulloch-Pitts (refs 11-14) as the origin of the
computation-in-the-brain model, then cites Kleene (ref 13) to show that the
computational framework is a mathematical abstraction, not a brain model.

## Abstract

No PubMed abstract; the article is pre-PubMed indexing. Crossref lists 10.1007/BF02478259
(issn 0007-4209, journal Bulletin of Mathematical Biophysics, 1943, volume 5, pages
115-133). The paper's argument is summarized in the anchor (refs 11-14) and in Kleene's
1956 paper, both cited there.

## Key findings

No quantitative extraction (1943 mathematics paper; the accessible abstract text
describes the theoretical framework). The key points from the abstract and secondary
summaries:

- The paper models neural activity as a logical calculus: "the all-or-none behaviour of
  neurons", "the number of synapses between any two neurons is a finite integer", and
  "the synaptic delay of any neuron is a finite integer".
- McCulloch-Pitts nets compute exactly the predicates definable in a restricted
  second-order logic (the "nervous activity" of the title).
- The 2024 reprint in Bulletin of Mathematical Biology carries the same volume and
  page range (5:115-133, Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 86: 27, 2024, DOI
  10.1007/s12561-024-09451-8) and a foreword on the paper's influence.
- The paper's influence: it inspired the computational theory of mind (Putnam, Fodor)
  and artificial neural networks (Rosenblatt 1958).

## Method

Mathematical logic (the first complete formal model of neural computation).

## Relevance to the core question

Anchor references 11-14 (Turing, McCulloch-Pitts, Kleene, Cobb) support the second
reason's premise that the computational view of mind is a mathematical abstraction,
not a brain model. The anchor's argument (ref 11-14) is not that computation is wrong
but that computation is not consciousness.

## Limitations and counterarguments

The McCulloch-Pitts model is not a biological model; it treats neurons as logical
units and ignores the biological features (neuromodulation, dendritic computation,
oscillation) that the anchor's second reason claims matter. Seth's target article
(see the Seth 2025 entry) makes this argument explicitly.
