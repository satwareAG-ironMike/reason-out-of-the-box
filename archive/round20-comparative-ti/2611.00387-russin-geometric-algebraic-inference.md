## Abstract (condensed)

Formalizes two inference strategies: geometric, which gradually shapes representational geometry to
reflect relational structure so later inference is cheap; and algebraic, which flexibly attends to
and reasons over novel relations given in context at extra inference-time cost. Identifies opposing
signatures of the two in transitive inference and characterizes when each is observed. Tests the
predictions across humans, neural networks trained from scratch, and large language models, with
causal interventions in the networks.

## Key findings (verified from abstract; venue verified from the arXiv record)

- Predicts the geometric strategy dominates for familiar relations and the algebraic strategy
  emerges for de novo inferences drawn in context.
- Reports opposing signatures of both strategies in humans, from-scratch neural networks and large
  language models.
- Causal interventions show geometric inference depends on representational geometry and algebraic
  on selective-attention mechanisms, reproducing the effects of analogous neurobiological
  disruptions in humans.
- Unifies human/LLM content effects and machine-learning data-distribution effects under one
  framework.

## Relevance to core question

This is the comparative-transitive-inference primary that connects the human/animal analogy to the
machine claim on a single controlled axis (issue #74 named it as the direct analogue of what the
study tries to show and must rule out). Its value is a design lesson for the thesis, not a positive
result: it shows that "passes transitive inference" is under-determined, because two mechanistically
opposite strategies produce the same behavioral pass and are separated only by representational and
attentional probes plus causal intervention. That is precisely the confound the study design carries
into its Condition 3 (process-level causal evidence) and into #71's depth ceiling. No acceptance
criterion is met (humans, from-scratch nets and undisclosed-data LLMs), but it supplies the
human-baseline experimental logic (opposing geometric/algebraic signatures) the M4 human arm needs.
Verdict: mixed - it gives the analogy a testable form while showing a behavioral pass alone is not
diagnostic.
