## Abstract (condensed)

Introduces PrOntoQA, a synthetic question-answering dataset where each example is generated from a
synthetic world model represented in first-order logic, so the generated chain-of-thought can be
parsed into symbolic proofs for formal analysis. The point is to test how models obtain answers and
whether they rely on heuristics rather than the chain they emit. Analysis is on InstructGPT and
GPT-3.

## Key findings (verified from abstract; venue verified from the arXiv record)

- Models make correct individual deduction steps and are generally capable of reasoning, even on
  fictional contexts.
- The failure mode is proof planning: when several valid deduction steps are available, the models
  cannot systematically explore the alternatives; they proceed greedily.
- Because the world model is symbolic, each answer carries a checkable proof, so the greedy failure
  is localized to a specific reasoning operation rather than asserted from accuracy alone.

## Relevance to core question

PrOntoQA is the second depth-controlled rule benchmark issue #74 named, and its contribution to the
thesis is the mechanism of failure rather than the ceiling. It is the peer-reviewed statement that
step-level inference is not the bottleneck - search over the proof is - which is exactly the
elicitation-versus-faculty distinction this repository draws across rounds 5 and 9 (validation and
control are not native). It bears directly on the "genuine reasoning versus associative shortcut"
dispute that round 8 and the comparative-TI literature raise: a model can produce a valid step at
every local choice and still not be running a sound global procedure. In-weights models here are
instruction-tuned and task-exposed, so the entry supports neither the Condition 1 nor the
Condition 3 requirement (process-level causal proof of an algorithm), and the finding is used as the
methodological control the study must clear. Verdict: mixed/balanced - local deduction intact, global
planning absent.
