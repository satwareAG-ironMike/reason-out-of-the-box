## Abstract (condensed)

Investigates the transitive reasoning of two architectures, LLaMA 2 and Flan-T5, by manipulating
facts in two compositional datasets (QASC and Bamboogle). Controls for cues that could drive
performance: word/phrase overlap across sections of the test input; the models' inherent knowledge
from pretraining or fine-tuning; and named entities.

## Key findings (verified from abstract; venue verified from the arXiv record)

- Both models exploit word/phrase overlap across input sections as a cue.
- Flan-T5 is more resilient than LLaMA 2 to the pretraining-knowledge and named-entity controls,
  showing less variance.
- The authors' reading: models may acquire an understanding of transitivity through fine-tuning on
  knowingly relevant datasets, a hypothesis left to future work.

## Relevance to core question

This is the closest published analogue, on the machine side, of the control problem the study design
must solve (issue #74): what distinguishes passing a transitive test from performing inference. It
shows that apparent transitive reasoning in instruction-tuned models is partly cued by surface
lexical overlap, not derived from the relation, which mirrors the "associative learning not logic"
reading of animal transitive inference archived this round. The architectures are fine-tuned and
pretrained on undisclosed data, so none of the three acceptance criteria is met; the value is the
control methodology (overlap, prior-knowledge and entity ablations) that the thesis must apply to
its own ladder scores. Verdict: opposes - transitive-closure passing without demonstrated inference,
with the confound identified rather than removed.
