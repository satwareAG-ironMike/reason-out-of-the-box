## Abstract (condensed)

A review of transitive inference (TI) in non-human animals: describes the tasks customarily used,
reviews the empirical findings, and critically analyzes the prominent models. TI has been
demonstrated in many species, and the signature human effects (serial position effect, symbolic
distance effect) appear in non-humans too. Models divide into cognitive ones (formal logic, mental
representation of premises) and associative ones (value transfer, reinforcement and
non-reinforcement).

## Key findings (verified from PubMed abstract; venue/DOI verified from the PubMed record)

- Concludes the reinforcement-based associative models are "in a much better empirical and
  theoretical position" than the cognitive ones.
- Argues TI in non-human animals "should be considered a property of reinforcement history rather
  than of inferential processes."
- Establishes that the behavioral signature shared with humans (serial position, symbolic distance)
  does not settle the mechanism question.
