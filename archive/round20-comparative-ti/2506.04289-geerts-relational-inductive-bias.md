## Abstract (condensed)

Studies how transformers perform transitive inference (A>B, B>C, therefore A>C), a relational
behavior from psychology. Compares in-weights learning (IWL) and in-context learning (ICL) on these
tasks and finds profoundly different generalization patterns. Uses a congruency paradigm to probe
the two generalization patterns in large language models without access to their training data.

## Key findings (verified from abstract; venue verified from the arXiv record)

- IWL models learn a linear embedding, which yields transitive inference plus other behavioral
  effects also seen in humans and animals.
- ICL models can generalize transitively, but only when the training data necessitates it;
  otherwise they learn a match-and-copy strategy.
- Pretraining ICL models on in-context linear-regression tasks that supply a latent linear
  representation makes their behavior and internal representations qualitatively and quantitatively
  more like IWL.
- Probing large language models with the congruency paradigm, IWL-style reasoning yields more
  transitive generalization than ICL; prompting models to use a linear mental map increases
  transitive inference across geometric prompts.

## Relevance to core question

This entry carries the mechanism this thesis most needs and had no evidence for (issue #74): it
separates a genuine transitive-closure representation (a linear map, IWL) from a shortcut
(match-and-copy, ICL), and shows the shortcut is the default unless the geometry is induced. That is
the machine version of the paired-associate critique in the comparative-TI literature archived the
same round. It supports neither acceptance criterion - the transformers are trained on the task
family and the LLMs have undisclosed data - but it supplies the diagnostic (congruency probing plus
linear-map prompting) that turns a ladder pass/fail into a statement about representation rather
than accuracy, exactly the process-level evidence the open-problem spec (Condition 3) demands.
Verdict: mixed - transitive generalization is real but training-regime-dependent, and the default
is the shortcut.
