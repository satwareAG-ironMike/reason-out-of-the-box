## Abstract (condensed)

Facts and rules are given as natural-language sentences, bypassing a formal representation; the
models (called RuleTakers) are trained on synthetically generated data to reason, or emulate
reasoning, over them. The paper gives the first empirical demonstration that this soft reasoning
over language is learnable, reaches high accuracy, and generalizes to test items needing
substantially deeper chaining than seen in training, and transfers to hand-authored and
paraphrased rulebases. The Entailment Bank is the dataset behind the study.

## Key findings (verified from abstract; venue verified from the arXiv record)

- Achieves high accuracy, 99% on the reported setting, and 95%+ scores on test data requiring
  chaining substantially deeper than the training depth.
- The depth claim is explicit and controlled: depth of the deduction chain is the varied axis, the
  same axis as the ladder family and issue #71's depth ceiling d*.
- Models transfer to two hand-authored rulebases and to rulebases paraphrased into more natural
  language.
- Positioning is deliberately limited: transformers as limited "soft theorem provers" operating
  over explicit theories in language.

## Relevance to core question

This is the published depth-controlled rule-reasoning benchmark that is the direct methodological
neighbour of the ladder family (issue #74 named it, alongside PrOntoQA). It is load-bearing for the
skeptical reading: RuleTaker's positive depth-generalization result is obtained by training on
synthetic data of that exact rule family, so it is the opposite of the thesis's Condition 1 (base
model, no task training). Read against this repository, it sharpens the open problem: the depth
ceiling this study measures (ladders, #71) is a real and transferable phenomenon, but the only
systems shown to generalize past trained depth on such tasks were trained on the task distribution.
It is also the control this study must answer for: high scores on depth-extended rule tests are not
by themselves evidence of out-of-the-box inference when task-family training is present. Verdict:
opposes (task-trained; depth generalization does not imply out-of-the-box reasoning).
