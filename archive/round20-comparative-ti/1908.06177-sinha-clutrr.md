## Abstract (condensed)

A diagnostic benchmark suite to clarify robustness and systematicity in NLU systems. Motivated by
classic inductive logic programming, CLUTRR requires a system to infer kinship relations between
characters in short stories. Success needs both extracting relations between entities and inferring
the logical rules governing them. Held-out combinations of logical rules measure systematic
generalization; curated noise facts measure robustness.

## Key findings (verified from abstract; venue verified from the arXiv record)

- State-of-the-art NLU models (BERT, MAC) show a substantial performance gap against a graph neural
  network that works directly with symbolic inputs.
- The symbolic graph-based model exhibits both stronger systematic generalization (unseen rule
  combinations) and greater robustness to injected noise facts.
- Performance is framed as composition of entity extraction plus rule induction, so the benchmark
  is built to separate the two.

## Relevance to core question

This is the machine-side analogue the archive lacked (issue #74): a published benchmark where a
relation must be composed over multiple hops from text, and where the depth of the required chain
is a controlled variable. It is the direct neighbour of the ladder family and of issue #71's depth
ceiling, but it argues the skeptical side of this repository's thesis, not the positive side: the
systems that generalize best are those operating on explicit symbolic structure, while the neural
sequence models that get accuracy are the ones that collapse on unseen rule combinations. Nothing
here is a base model reasoning out of the box; models are trained on the task family, and the
compositional gain belongs to the graph model, not to a pretrained transformer. Verdict: opposes,
in the specific sense that compositionality on this relational benchmark tracks the symbolic
representation rather than out-of-the-box neural reasoning.
