# A Careful Examination of Large Language Model Performance on Grade School Arithmetic (GSM1k)

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| arXiv | [2405.00332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332) |
| Authors | Hugh Zhang, Jeff Da, et al. (Scale AI) |
| Venue | **NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks** |
| Year | 2024 |
| Archive round | 2 - Skeptical / counter-evidence |
| Archived | 2026-09-02 |

## Abstract (condensed)
Builds GSM1k: 1,205 human-authored grade-school problems matched to GSM8k on human solve
rate, steps, answer magnitude. Evaluates leading open and closed models on both.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Accuracy drops up to **8%** GSM8k -> GSM1k in some model families (Phi, Mistral);
  systematic overfitting across almost all sizes in those families.
- Overfitting correlates with memorization: Spearman r=0.36 (p=0.03) between a model's
  per-character log-likelihood on GSM8k and its GSM8k-GSM1k gap.
- **Crucial counter-nuance (verified in-body)**: "all frontier models show minimal signs of
  overfitting"; even the most overfit models still solved novel problems (Phi-2: >50% on
  guaranteed-unseen GSM1k). Authors: "sufficiently strong LLMs also learn elementary reasoning
  ability during training" and "overfit models are still capable of reasoning."
- Goodhart evidence: leaderboard-optimized models dropped sharply.

## Relevance to core question
**Cuts both ways - the most balanced paper in the archive.** Confirms contamination is real
for some models, but its own data refute the strong "it's all memorization" thesis: models
guaranteed to have never seen these exact problems still solve most of them.
