# GSM-Symbolic: Understanding the Limitations of Mathematical Reasoning in Large Language Models

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| arXiv | [2410.05229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05229) |
| Authors | Seyed-Iman Mirzadeh et al. (Apple) |
| Venue | **ICLR 2025** |
| Year | 2024/2025 |
| Archive round | 2 - Skeptical / counter-evidence |
| Archived | 2026-09-02 |

## Abstract (condensed)
Introduces GSM-Symbolic: templates generate unlimited symbolic variants of GSM8k problems,
so every test item is fresh. Evaluates 20+ LLMs across model scales and releases.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Performance **drops on average ~10%** vs GSM8k, with high variance across instantiations of
  the *same* problem - the model's answer depends on irrelevant numbers.
- Adding a single clause that is semantically irrelevant to the solution causes drops up to
  65% (GSM-NoOp).
- Conclusion: models "reproduce reasoning patterns from training" rather than executing a
  stable solving algorithm.

## Relevance to core question
**Strong skeptic evidence on robustness.** True out-of-the-box reasoning should be invariant
to harmless rephrasing; LLMs are not. Caveat: subsequent evaluations (2025-2026 frontier
models, e.g. on revisits of GSM-Symbolic) show substantially smaller NoOp sensitivity,
suggesting the gap narrows with scale and training but does not vanish.
