## Abstract (condensed)
Proposes that LLM behavior is best understood through the *autoregressive next-token
objective* they are trained on. Predicts sensitivity to three task properties: probability of
the answer, frequency of task instances in training data, and probability of individual
tokens - factors that should be irrelevant to a true reasoner.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Across the evaluated task suite and models including GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, performance systematically depends on answer/task probability
  - even in GPT-4. Rare answers and infrequent task variants break otherwise-solved problems.
- The failures are *predictable* from the training objective - a unifying account of
  both impressive successes and surprising failures.

## Relevance to core question
**Strong skeptic framing.** If reasoning were box-fresh, accuracy should depend on the
logical structure of the instance, not on how probable the surface forms are. The observed
probability sensitivity is direct behavioral evidence for pattern-completion explanations,
at least for models of that generation.
