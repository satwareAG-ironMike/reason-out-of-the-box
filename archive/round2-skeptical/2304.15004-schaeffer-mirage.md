# Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a Mirage?

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|

| Authors | Rylan Schaeffer, Brando Miranda, Sanmi Koyejo (Stanford) |

| Year | 2023 |
| Archive round | 2 - Skeptical / counter-evidence |
| Archived | 2026-09-02 |

## Abstract (condensed)
Proposes an alternative explanation for emergent abilities: for a fixed task and model family,
emergence appears due to the researcher's choice of **discontinuous metric** (e.g. exact match
accuracy). With nonlinear/discontinuous metrics, smooth predictable improvement looks like a
sudden phase transition; with continuous metrics or better statistical analysis, emergence
largely disappears.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Both claims of the emergence thesis (sharpness + unpredictability) are metric artifacts in
  many published cases.
- Authors demonstrate they can *induce* apparent emergence or its absence at will by choosing
  the metric - a strong methodological indictment.
- Follow-ups: "Understanding Emergent Abilities from the Loss Perspective" (arXiv:2403.15796)
  shows many abilities are predictable functions of pretraining loss, not model scale.

## Relevance to core question
**Core skeptic paper.** Does not show LLMs cannot reason; shows that the *scaling-curve*
evidence for "new reasoning appears at scale" is unreliable. Shifts the burden of proof from
scaling curves to controlled tasks and process-level evidence - exactly what is still missing.
