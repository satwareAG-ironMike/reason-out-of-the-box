## Abstract (condensed)
Investigates transformer limits on three compositional tasks: multi-digit multiplication,
logic grid puzzles, and dynamic programming. Models task execution as computation graphs and
tests whether LLMs solve tasks by composing graph nodes or by matching linearized subgraphs
seen in training.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Evidence that transformers behave like **linearized subgraph/path matchers** rather than
  executors of systematic procedures.
- Accuracy collapses toward zero when task complexity exceeds the depth of patterns observed
  during training; theoretical analysis shows error compounds with the number of steps.
- "Faith": performance where the reasoning path exists in training. "Fate": collapse where it
  does not - even on structurally identical problems one step deeper.

## Relevance to core question
**Core skeptic paper on mechanism.** Peer-reviewed demonstration that reported reasoning
success can be produced by sophisticated retrieval over training paths. Sets the empirical
criterion any pro-reasoning paper must meet: generalization to strictly deeper/novel
composition, which matched-path models cannot pass.
