## Abstract

Condensed from the PubMed record. Abstract-level verification, not full-text verified.
Insects are traditionally thought to respond to noxious stimuli in an inflexible manner
devoid of context-dependent modulation. The paper tests whether bumblebees' attraction to
high sucrose concentrations reduces their avoidance of noxiously heated feeders.

## Key findings

- Bees chose between unheated or noxiously heated (55 °C) feeders that differed in sucrose
  concentration and were marked by color.
- Bees avoided the noxious feeders when unheated feeders carried high sucrose; as unheated
  feeder sucrose concentration decreased, feeding from noxious feeders increased: a
  motivational trade-off of nociceptive responses.
- The trade-off used learned color cues, so the information mediating the prioritization is
  processed by the brain, not only peripherally.
- The authors conclude bumblebees can use contextual information to modulate nociceptive
  responses, "consistent with a capacity for pain experiences in insects" (abstract wording).
- Erratum: PNAS 122(37): e2522767122 (2025-09-16, PMID 40928884) corrected the reported
  interaction P value from 0.039 to 0.033. The claim was later contested and reassessed;
  see entries 11, 12, 13.

## Relevance to core question

The most contested claim in the bee chain: context-dependent modulation of nociception in an
insect brain. Its full adversarial record (claim, critique, erratum, reassessment, response)
is the strongest public example of indicator-based sentience evidence being audited, and it
is the methodological template the claim ledger applies to machine indicators
(docs/claims-insect-sentience.md). Not reasoning evidence; overall verdict unchanged.
