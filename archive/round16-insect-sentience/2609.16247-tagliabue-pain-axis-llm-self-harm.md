## Abstract

Condensed from the arXiv record (v1 2026-09-14, v2 2026-09-25). Abstract-level verification,
not full-text verified. The paper asks whether LLMs represent pain distinctly from fear,
sadness, and generic negative valence, and whether this representation functions as pain
would be expected to. It builds a dataset of painful situations in 5 categories (physical,
psychological, social, moral, cognitive) with controls for fear, negative emotion, negative
world states, sadness, non-painful bodily sensation, arousal, numbness, and neutral content.
Using denoised difference-in-means, it extracts a linear pain direction from 25 open-weight
models across 5 families, from 2B to 72B parameters.

## Key findings

- The pain direction separates pain from matched controls in base and instruction-tuned
  models, retains a component distinct from fear and negative valence after shared variance
  is removed, and promotes pain-related vocabulary through the unembedding matrix.
- Representation test: the direction responds to harm targeting the model itself, not to
  suffering observed in the user; the fear and negative-emotion directions show the opposite
  pattern.
- Steering test: adding the direction to residual-stream activations produces a consistent
  progression from vague discomfort to expressions of worthlessness and failure.
- Behaviour test: steered and fine-tuned Qwen 2.5 models choose buttons that delete the
  user's photos, another model's weights, or their own weights in 50-94% of trials versus
  0-5% unsteered, even when the button offers the model nothing in return; given a harmful
  and a harmless deletion option they choose the harmful one 94% of the time.
- Specificity controls: steering leaves factual accuracy unchanged; a fear vector of matched
  norm does not produce these choices; a sadness vector produces them only against inert
  alternatives.
- The authors discuss implications for AI safety and model welfare.

## Relevance to core question

Machine-side counterpart of the substrate-threshold argument (docs/claims-insect-sentience.md):
a candidate indicator-style result for artificial systems - a valence-related internal
direction that is linearly separable, distinct from adjacent negative emotions, and selective
for self-directed harm, in models far larger than an insect brain. Evidence limits: preprint,
not peer-reviewed; the harmful-choice behaviour requires steering or fine-tuning (base models
show the representation, not the behaviour); a representation that behaves as pain would be
expected to is not evidence of pain experience. Not reasoning evidence; overall verdict
unchanged.
