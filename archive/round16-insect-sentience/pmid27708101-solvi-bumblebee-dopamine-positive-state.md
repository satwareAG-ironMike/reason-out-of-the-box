## Abstract

Condensed from the PubMed record. Abstract-level verification, not full-text verified.
Whether invertebrates exhibit positive emotion-like states and what mechanisms underlie such
states was poorly understood. The paper demonstrates dopamine-dependent positive
emotion-like states in bumblebees across behavioral contexts.

## Key findings

- After training with one rewarding and one unrewarding cue, bees that received pretest
  sucrose responded in a positive manner toward ambiguous cues (optimistic-like bias).
- In a second experiment, pretest consumption of sucrose solution resulted in a shorter time
  to reinitiate foraging after a simulated predator attack.
- Both behavioral changes were abolished with topical application of the dopamine antagonist
  fluphenazine.
- Control: pretest sucrose does not simply cause bees to become more exploratory.
- The authors present the findings as a new opportunity for understanding the fundamental
  neural elements of emotions and as a change in the view of how emotion states affect
  decision-making in animals.

## Relevance to core question

Positive-valence counterpart to Bateson 2011 (entry 2): bidirectional, neuromodulator-linked
affective state in an insect brain. Dopamine's conserved role in reward processing is the
neural-continuity premise the substrate-threshold argument leans on
(docs/claims-insect-sentience.md). Not reasoning evidence; overall verdict unchanged.
