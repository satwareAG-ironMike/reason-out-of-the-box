## Abstract

Condensed from the publisher abstract (Crossref/OpenAlex records). Abstract-level
verification, not full-text verified. Object play (interaction with inanimate objects with no
apparent immediate function) is documented mainly in mammals and birds. Whether insects
engage in play had not been systematically tested. The paper analyzes spontaneous ball
rolling in bumble bees (Bombus terrestris) against behavioral criteria for play.

## Key findings

- Ball rolling meets five criteria for play: (1) it did not contribute to immediate survival
  strategies, (2) it was intrinsically rewarding, (3) it differed in form from functional
  behaviour, (4) it was repetitive but not stereotyped, and (5) it was initiated under
  low-stress conditions.
- Alternative explanations (food exploration, nest tidying, mating-related behaviour) were
  examined and rejected.
- Age and sex differences mirror vertebrate play patterns: younger bees rolled more balls
  than older bees, and male bees engaged longer with individual balls than females.

## Relevance to core question

Play is a candidate sentience indicator in an insect brain, and the five criteria are the
template the claim ledger translates into artificial-agent tests
(docs/claims-insect-sentience.md): intrinsically motivated, non-functional, repetitive
behaviour as an observable signature. Not reasoning evidence; overall verdict unchanged.
