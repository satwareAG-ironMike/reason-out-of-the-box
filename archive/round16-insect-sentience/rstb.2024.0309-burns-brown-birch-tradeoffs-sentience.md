## Abstract

Condensed from the publisher abstract (Crossref/OpenAlex records). Abstract-level
verification, not full-text verified. Motivational trade-off behaviours, where an organism
behaves as if flexibly weighing an opportunity for reward against a risk of injury, are
often regarded as evidence that the organism has valenced experiences like pain. This type
of evidence has been influential in shifting opinion regarding crabs and insects. The paper
is part of the theme issue "Evolutionary functions of consciousness".

## Key findings

- Critics note: (i) the precise links between trade-offs and consciousness are not fully
  known; (ii) simple trade-offs are evinced by the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans, mediated
  by a mechanism plausibly too simple to support conscious experience; (iii) pain can
  sometimes interfere with rather than support making trade-offs rationally.
- The authors argue these cases do not undermine trade-off evidence in general but show that
  the nature of the trade-off, and its underlying neural substrate, matter; they investigate
  precisely how.

## Relevance to core question

The philosophical hinge of the bee controversy, directly transferable to the machine side:
indicator evidence depends on the substrate implementing the indicator, which is exactly the
question the pain-axis preprint (entry 1) probes for LLMs (docs/claims-insect-sentience.md).
Not reasoning evidence; overall verdict unchanged.
