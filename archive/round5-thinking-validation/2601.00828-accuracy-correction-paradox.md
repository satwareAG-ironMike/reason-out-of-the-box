## Abstract (condensed)
Decomposes self-correction into three sub-capabilities: error detection, error localization,
error correction. Cross-model experiments on GSM8K-Complex (n=500 per model, 346 errors)
find an Accuracy-Correction Paradox: weaker models detect and correct more of the errors
they actually make; stronger models leave more errors undetected.

## Key findings
- Self-correction is not one skill: detection, localization, and fixing can dissociate.
- Strong models' confidence in fluent wrong answers suppresses detection - a calibrated
  feeling of correctness is anti-correlated with catching errors.
- Error "depth" (how deep in the reasoning chain the fault sits) predicts correctability.

## Relevance to core question
**2026 refinement of the validation story**: what limits self-correction is not lack of
fixing ability but lack of *detection*, and detection is confounded by fluency-driven
confidence. Directly connects the faithfulness literature (round 3) with the
self-correction literature (this round).
