## Abstract (condensed)
Critically examines intrinsic self-correction: an LLM judging and fixing its own reasoning
using no external feedback. Finds it consistently fails and often degrades accuracy.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Without oracle labels, self-correction **lowers accuracy**: GPT-4-Turbo GSM8K 91.5 -> 88.0
  (round 1); Llama-2 collapses 62.0 -> 36.5 over two rounds.
- On GSM8K, GPT-3.5 keeps its initial answer 74.7% of the time; among changes, it flips
  correct->incorrect more often than incorrect->correct.
- Authors' diagnosis: "the fundamental issue is that LLMs cannot properly judge the
  correctness of their reasoning."
- Part of Self-Refine's reported gains traced to prompt-design artifacts: adding an explicit
  instruction ("include ALL concepts") to the *initial* prompt beats the whole
  self-correction pipeline.

## Relevance to core question
**Core negative result on validation ability.** An LLM does not natively know how to
validate its own ideas: without external ground truth (tools, tests, humans), "checking" is
just another generation step with its own error rate. Self-validation must be engineered
(execution, verifiers) or trained (RL), not assumed.
