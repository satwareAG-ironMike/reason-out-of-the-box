## Abstract (condensed)
Studies whether LMs can evaluate the validity of their own claims and predict which
questions they will answer correctly. Larger models are well-calibrated on multiple-choice
and true/false formats; for open-ended tasks, models can first propose answers, then assign
a probability "P(True)" that each answer is correct, with encouraging calibration and
scaling.

## Key findings
- Calibration is format-dependent and scales with size: a model can be well calibrated on
  one task format and poorly on another.
- P(True) self-evaluation works far better than asking the model directly "is this true?"
  in one shot - self-evaluation is a separate skill from answering.
- Self-knowledge is partial and statistical (confidence ranking), not logical verification.

## Relevance to core question
**The calibration half of "how do LLMs validate ideas."** Models carry a usable internal
confidence signal that can rank their own candidate answers - the raw material for
validation - but it is a correlational signal, not a proof procedure. Pairs with Huang
(ICLR 2024): confidence can rank ideas; it cannot reliably falsify them.
