## Abstract (condensed)
Tests the same creativity intervention on humans and LLMs: cross-domain mapping (drawing
inspiration from a random remote source domain) for novel product designs. Compares
originality and usefulness of outputs across species.

## Key findings
- The intervention that reliably boosts human creative originality does not transfer
  unchanged to LLMs: forcing remote analogies helps humans; LLMs benefit differently or
  not at all, depending on how the mapping is prompted.
- Evidence that the *mechanisms* of unique idea production differ, even when outputs
  score similarly on tests.

## Relevance to core question
**Mechanism-level disanalogy for the biological comparison**: human uniqueness comes partly
from remote analogical retrieval; LLM similarity scores do not respond to the same lever.
Similar test scores, different generator.
