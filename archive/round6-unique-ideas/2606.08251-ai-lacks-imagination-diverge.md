## Abstract (condensed)
Largest scientist-in-the-loop idea evaluation: authors of 121,640 recent preprints
(biology, medicine, chemistry, social science) judged LLM-generated hypotheses derived from
their own papers. 6,749 scientists returned 25,139 rating sets on novelty, feasibility,
probability of truth, and adoption favorability (1-9 scales, after a rubric comprehension
test).

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- 26 LLMs tested: 19 non-reasoning chat models, 5 reasoning models, 2 agentic deep-research
  systems, across 8 providers. Each author rated the 5 most semantically distinct
  hypotheses for their paper, stratified across reasoning and non-reasoning models.
- **"Non-reasoning LLMs collapse onto a narrow region of the idea space."**
- **"Reasoning models, by contrast, diverge from one another, from non-reasoning models,
  and from humans"** and occupy a more diverse region; the gain "is not driven solely by
  parameter counts."
- Negation failure holds for all models: every LLM formulates null hypotheses (claims of
  no relationship) less often than human scientists, detected by a 99.5%-accuracy
  classifier.
- Rater effect documented: "Scientists discount novelty, prefer their own ideas, and split
  by field and seniority" - the human baseline is itself biased toward its own work.

## Relevance to core question
**Nuanced, not one-sided.** Frame-locking is a *non-reasoning-model* property; reasoning
models genuinely diverge. What no LLM does is negate - propose that the expected effect is
absent - the scientific move that requires modeling the world rather than the literature.
Uniqueness through divergence: partially demonstrated. Uniqueness through negation: not.
