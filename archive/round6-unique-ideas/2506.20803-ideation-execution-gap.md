## Abstract (condensed)
Tests whether LLM-generated ideas lead to better research when actually executed: 43 expert
researchers executed randomly assigned ideas (LLM-generated vs human-expert), writing full
SRs under blind conditions; execution quality scored by separate blinded expert reviewers.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- When novelty-of-plan meets reality, the LLM advantage narrows or inverts on
  execution-dependent metrics: LLM ideas score significantly worse on the primary
  execution-outcome measures than human ideas.
- Confirms the "ideation-execution gap": judged novelty is a weak predictor of research
  outcome quality.
- Statistical novelty without executability is cheap; the market for unique ideas prices
  in execution.

## Relevance to core question
**The reality check on "LLMs come up with unique ideas."** Yes - ideas that *read* as novel
(and win blind reviews); no - ideas that survive contact with execution. Uniqueness so far
is surface-level more often than humans', reinforcing the diversity weakness found by the
parent study.
