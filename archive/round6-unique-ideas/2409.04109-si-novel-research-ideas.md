# Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas? A Large-Scale Human Study with 100+ NLP Researchers

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| arXiv | [2409.04109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109) |
| Authors | Chenglei Si, Diyi Yang, Tatsunori Hashimoto (Stanford) |
| Venue | **ICLR 2025** |
| Year | 2024/2025 |
| Archive round | 6 - Unique idea generation |
| Archived | 2026-09-03 |

## Abstract (condensed)
First head-to-head comparison of LLM-generated vs human-expert research ideas under a
controlled protocol: 100+ NLP researchers wrote ideas and blind-reviewed ~300 writeups
across topics, with confounders (area, format, evaluation) controlled.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- **AI ideas judged more novel than human-expert ideas** (p<0.05, robust across multiple
  statistical tests and hypothesis corrections).
- Caveats, by the authors' own analysis: human participants self-rated their submitted
  ideas as ~median (top 43%) of their lifetime ideas; reviewer overall scores correlate
  with novelty (r=0.725) and excitement (r=0.854) but barely with feasibility (r=0.097) -
  judges may reward flashy novelty.
- LLM ideation **lacks diversity at scale** ("lack idea diversity when we scale up idea
  generation") and LLMs "cannot currently serve as reliable evaluators".

## Relevance to core question
**Strongest peer-reviewed evidence that LLMs can produce ideas humans rate as unique.**
Also documents the two structural weaknesses: statistical novelty without diversity, and no
self-evaluation ability - which motivates the execution test in the follow-up paper.
