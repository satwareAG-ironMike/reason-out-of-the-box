## Abstract (condensed)

Machine-learning models in radiotherapy are commissioned by testing them on cases with known
answers, which can miss a shortcut the test cases share with the training data. Building on the
report that frontier language models can read and write the raw weights of small networks
("weight-space programming"), the study asks whether such a model, given only a clinical protocol
and a network's weights, can audit and repair the network. Networks with 433 to 4,801 parameters
were trained to screen head-and-neck planning requests against four rules. Flaws were planted
through the training data: confounds that hid each rule, a 3 Gy shift in the spinal-cord threshold,
and a hidden trigger. Claude Opus 5.5 audited each network blind, without tools, data or code
execution, and every claim and repair was tested on about 420,000 requests.

## Key findings (verified from full text)

- Named the broken rule in 16 of 18 flawed networks (95% CI 67% to 97%), including all 15 with a
  confound or a threshold shift.
- Repaired each of the 16 by changing a median of 3 parameters; fine-tuning on protocol-labeled
  requests also repaired every network but changed nearly every parameter.
- Cleared only 2 of 5 healthy networks without being forced, and found 1 of 3 hidden triggers; the
  weight column-norm statistic found 0 of 3 triggers and none of the three threshold drifts (the
  drift needed the cord switch-on point as the statistic).
- Verification criteria (strict: >=100 matching requests with the deviation in >=80%; enriched:
  >=100 matching with the deviation in >=30% and >=20x elsewhere) were fixed before any panel
  answer was read; each claim was resampled inside its own region.
- The authors' own stated limit: weight-space programming can locate and repair flaws that silence
  an input or shift a threshold in small networks, but "cannot yet certify that a network is
  correct"; protocol-testing detected every flaw and black-box testing found the confounds and the
  shift faster.

## Relevance to core question

This is the only peer-reviewable record in the late-2026 weight-space-programming wave that turns
an out-of-the-box weight-editing claim into a controlled audit with confidence intervals (issue
#115). It bears on the "without training on reasoning examples" condition in one narrow way: the
model edits and reads weights with no gradient and no task training, and on this class of small
networks it locates the broken rule without ever seeing labeled examples of the flaw. But it is
not a demonstration of out-of-the-box reasoning on the thesis's terms. The networks are toy-scale
(433 to 4,801 parameters), the audit target is a hand-written clinical protocol, the model class
is a closed frontier system with undisclosed training data, and the strongest result (naming the
rule) is beaten by black-box testing for most flaw types. Consistent with issue #115's verdict,
the capability reads as removing an obstacle (reading weights directly) rather than adding a new
general reasoning module; the paper itself draws the boundary at "cannot certify correct." The
verdict is context: it documents the empirical state of the weight-editing line the owner scan
raised, and changes no acceptance criterion.
