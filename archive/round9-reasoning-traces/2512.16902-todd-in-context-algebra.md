## Abstract (condensed)
Trains transformers to solve arithmetic over sequences whose tokens are pure variables -
the assignment of tokens to algebraic elements varies from sequence to sequence, so no
fixed token embedding can encode the solution. Models reach near-perfect accuracy and
generalize to unseen groups; targeted causal data distributions isolate the mechanisms
they learn.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Task: arithmetic sampled from mixtures of finite groups (e.g., cyclic, dihedral);
  token-to-element assignment varies per sequence, so meaning exists only in-context.
  Transformers achieve near-perfect accuracy and generalize to unseen groups.
- Causal mechanism tests isolate three consistently learned mechanisms: commutative
  copying (a dedicated head copies answers), identity element recognition
  (distinguishing identity-containing facts), and closure-based cancellation (tracking
  group membership to constrain valid answers).
- Authors' framing: "models can develop symbolic reasoning mechanisms when trained to
  reason in-context about variables whose meanings are not fixed"; learned strategies
  depend on task structure.
- Code and data public (algebra.baulab.info). Peer-review: ICLR 2026 (arXiv comments;
  ICLR 2026 poster recording).

## Relevance to core question
Strongest current demonstration of procedure acquisition under per-prompt randomized
token semantics: the circuits are symbolic, mechanism-level (causally tested), and
embedding-independent. Boundary: models are trained on the task family - "in-context"
means within-sequence generality, not out-of-the-box generality. Feeds the thesis H1
frame (what algorithm-like computation transformers can implement) alongside Garg 2022
and Akyurek 2023.
