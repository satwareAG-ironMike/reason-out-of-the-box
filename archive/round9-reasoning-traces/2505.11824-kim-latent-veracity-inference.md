## Abstract (condensed)
Augments each reasoning step in a chain of thought with a latent veracity (correctness)
variable. Veracity Search (VS) performs otherwise intractable posterior inference over
veracity assignments using the LM's joint likelihood over veracity and final answer as a
proxy reward; VS outputs serve as pseudo-labels to fine-tune an Amortized Veracity
Inference (AVI) machine that then detects erroneous steps zero-shot and supports
self-correction.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Benchmarks with synthetic corruption: ProntoQA (first-order logic, depth 1-5, each
  step negated with probability 0.5), GSM8K (GPT-4.1 reference chains, numerals
  perturbed so exactly 50% of statements are incorrect), CommonsenseQA (negation p=0.5).
- VS reliably identifies erroneous steps across all three; AVI achieves comparable
  zero-shot accuracy without conditioning on the final answer.
- Self-correction use: negating steps predicted incorrect (ProntoQA) or appending
  predicted veracity labels (CommonsenseQA) improves the model's own chains.
- Peer-review: ICLR 2026 conference poster; official implementation released
  (github.com/alstn12088/veracity_inference).
- What it is NOT: "zero-shot" means no in-context demonstrations at test time, not an
  untrained verifier. The capability is engineered (inference-time search plus
  supervised fine-tuning on search-derived pseudo-labels), built on top of pretrained
  LMs with undisclosed training data.

## Relevance to core question
Strongest peer-reviewed demonstration that reasoning-error detection is achievable, but
only with machinery external to the model's native generation: explicit search over
correctness assignments plus a trained amortized verifier. Directly contrasts with the
archive's negative native-self-correction result (Huang et al., round 5): verification
works when it is engineered, not when assumed natively. No out-of-the-box claim.
