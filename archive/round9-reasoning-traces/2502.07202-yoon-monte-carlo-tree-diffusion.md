## Abstract (condensed)
Integrates diffusion-based planning with Monte Carlo Tree Search: denoising is
reconceptualized as a tree-structured process in which partially denoised plans are
iteratively evaluated, pruned, and refined. On long-horizon tasks MCTD outperforms
diffusion baselines and yields higher-quality solutions as inference-time computation
increases, recovering MCTS-style exploration-exploitation control inside a diffusion
planner.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Test-time compute scaling: as the inference budget grows, MCTD reaches near-perfect
  success on giant PointMaze tasks, while single-shot Diffuser and random-search
  variants yield only modest returns.
- Greedy tree-search ablation (no statistical search, no backtracking): 0% on giant
  mazes at 5-20 children per node; MCTD solves the same tasks at 100%.
- Component ablation on PointMaze (medium/large/giant): MCTD without causal denoising
  87/78/18 vs full MCTD 100/98/100; Diffusion Forcing baseline 40/55/34. Both causal
  denoising and tree search contribute, with the largest gains on the longest horizons.
- Authors' own framing: System 2-style deliberation is a built-in mechanism; it remains
  computationally expensive, and deciding when to plan (System 2) vs act model-free
  (System 1) is an open challenge.
- Peer-review: ICML 2025 (PMLR 267:72618-72640). Bengio co-author.

## Relevance to core question
Peer-reviewed evidence that explicit planning machinery improves long-horizon plans with
inference-time compute - but the search apparatus is designed, not emergent. This is the
attribution boundary the archive cares about: a built-in MCTS-over-diffusion loop says
nothing about an unassisted forward pass discovering such search. Supports the
"elicitation and scaffolding, not creation" reading of the field.
