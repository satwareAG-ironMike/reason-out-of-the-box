## Abstract (condensed)
Tests the limits of latent (unexternalized) reasoning: can models discover multi-step
planning strategies without supervision on intermediate steps and execute them within a
single forward pass? Using graph path-finding tasks that precisely control the number of
required latent planning steps, models show a striking depth limitation unresolved by
massive scaling, with a dissociation between discovering a latent strategy under
final-answer-only supervision and executing it once discovered. The authors argue such
limits lend credence to chain-of-thought monitoring.

## Key findings (verified from full text)
- Latent Planning Complexity (LPC): tiny transformers trained from scratch discover
  strategies of up to **3 latent steps**, failing abruptly at 4+; more attention heads
  do not overcome this.
- Fine-tuned GPT-4o and Qwen3-32B reach **4-5 latent steps**; GPT-5.4 under few-shot
  prompting (hidden reasoning trace disabled) attains **7**.
- Discovery ceiling vs generalization ceiling: the deepest strategy learnable during
  training is 5 latent steps, but once discovered it generalizes to **8** latent steps
  at test time - execution outstrips discovery.
- Finding 8: dense supervision bypasses the bottleneck - explicitly teaching the
  planning strategy removes it, isolating strategy discovery under sparse
  final-answer-only supervision as the bottleneck (not representational capacity).
- Scale does not dissolve the ceiling: from a 1.6M-parameter transformer to GPT-4o,
  discovered depth rises by only about 2 steps; few-shot closely tracks fine-tuned
  ceilings in models where both are measurable.
- Limitations stated: single (planning) domain; closed-source fine-tuning restricted by
  API costs, so GPT-5.4 rests on few-shot as a lower-bound proxy; more data might push
  the ceiling.
- Peer-review: COLM 2026 (published 2026-07-08).

## Relevance to core question
Quantifies, in a peer-reviewed setting, how hard it is to reason latently (without
externalized steps) even after massive pretraining and fine-tuning - the mirror image of
"reasoning out of the box": shallow unsupervised latent strategies exist, deep ones must
be taught or externalized. Aligns with the TC0/length-generalization theory in round 3
and the elicitation-only reading of rounds 1 and 4. If it holds beyond planning, deep
reasoning requires externalized computation, which is the project's open H1 territory.
