# Nightly firing 2026-11-21 04:40: `d_hat` on a pool endpoint, and what a 512-token cap has to do with it

Nightly round 79 in the runner's count, pool slot 2/6, a flash-class self-hosted endpoint
(serving build pinned as `model_digest` `4075b5949b2b0487`). Consciousness-studies tracker
issue #65; the loop objective is issue #195.

**Naming.** This firing is nightly round 79, but it does not reuse the `r79` label that two
artifacts already carry: `research/nightly-2026-11-21-0400/gate.json` on `main` (a staged
cross-ask gate whose JSON says `"round": 79`) and `explore/2026-11-21-r79-cross-ask/` on the
lane branch (PR #198). It also does not open an `explore/` directory: the lane layout and
`explore/LEDGER.md` exist only on the unmerged `explore-layout` branch, and a second LEDGER
written from `main` would collide with PR #198 on contact. So the round lives here, named by its
firing timestamp, and the label collision is filed as a finding.

## Question

What is `d_hat`, the frozen judge's measured trained depth, for this endpoint on the `ladders`
family, under the frozen calibration definition (`evaluation/frozen/rule.json` `trained_depth`:
`calibration_n` 40, `calibration_seed_base` 20260101, mastery 0.80)? See
[HYPOTHESIS.md](HYPOTHESIS.md) for why this round was picked and what would make it worthless.

## Method

[INSTRUMENT.md](INSTRUMENT.md) is the full contract. Short form: 40 items (8 per level at
`k` = 1, 2, 4, 6, 8) generated by `generators/ladders.py` through the frozen judge
(`verify.py` at commit `634d7fa`, pinned by sha256 `40b6a6eb...`; that path is not on `main`
yet - issue #194, PR #197 is owner-merge only - so the runner takes it from `FROZEN_JUDGE_DIR`
and refuses any other bytes). Prompts built by the frozen `hop_chain_yes_no_v1` template: rule
sentence, facts, one question, answer format, nothing else. Temperature 0.0, `max_tokens` 512,
sequential dispatch in round-robin order over levels. Scored on the visible answer field
against the judge's independent BFS solver, never the trace (issue #94).

`calibrate.py --selftest`: 26 of 26 green before any prompt, including the solver negative
control (cut the first hop of a `yes` item, the judge's gold must flip), planted censoring and
refusal records, and the Wilson arithmetic. Pre-dispatch: the #67 permutation-calibrated leak
gate ran on exactly this pool (`leak_gate.json`), gate blob `40d238b46907`.

40 of 40 prompts dispatched, 0 transport errors, 0 retries, 0 items stopped by the dispatch
budget, dispatch wall 845.4 s. One build throughout (`summary.json` lists exactly one digest).

## Finding 1: at this sampler cap, `d_hat` measures the token budget, not the trained depth

Across all 40 records, **zero answers were wrong**: the census is 34 `delivered_correct` and 0
`delivered_wrong`, at every level including `k` = 8. Every shortfall from 1.00 is a record that
never reached the answer field: `finish_reason: length` with an empty content field, which the
frozen census calls `not_delivered` (issue #145), and never a miss.

The mechanism is visible in the token column. The model's native trace grows with the chain it
has to walk - mean completion tokens 74, 117, 249, 397, 457 at `k` = 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, roughly 55
tokens per hop - and the trace channel carries 1,467 to 1,627 characters on the six capped
records. So a fixed budget of 512 tokens stops being enough at exactly the depth the calibration
is supposed to read, and depth and censoring are the same measurement at that cap. Consequences:

- `d_hat` = 6 on this build is not a property of the model. The same pool on the same build at a
  larger cap would give a different `d_hat`: the counterfactual is not measured here and is not
  claimed, but the direction is fixed by the census - the records that failed were never shown
  to be wrong, only never allowed to finish.
- The frozen rule fixes `calibration_n`, `calibration_seed_base` and the mastery accuracy, and
  says nothing about the sampler budget for the calibration arm. Two firings of the same model
  at two caps therefore report two different trained depths for one model, and both are
  rule-compliant. That is a comparability hole in `rule.json` as drafted, filed against #194.
- The lane's own convention already has the fix: a frozen pre-gate census (the `measure.json`
  shape used by the 2026-11-20 rounds) measured per family, and `max_tokens` frozen per family
  from it, with the calibration reported under both denominators and a censoring ceiling above
  which the level is `not_evaluable` rather than low.

## Finding 2: the calibration pool at the frozen `n` cannot carry exclusivity either

Two independent small-`n` facts, both measured before dispatch:

- The #67 leak gate reports, at 8 items per level, `min_detectable_accuracy` 1.0 at every depth
  level and `null_p95_accuracy` 0.875 at four of the five levels. That is, at the frozen
  calibration `n` the permutation gate can only reject a shortcut hypothesis that is *perfect*,
  while the calibration's own mastery bar is 0.80: a level can pass mastery on a pool the gate
  cannot clear of a leak at that `n`. The verdicts themselves were clean (`no_leak` at `k` = 2,
  4, 6, 8, permutation `p` = 0.4551 / 0.1329 / 1.0 / 0.3056 against 300 permutations; `k` = 1 is
  the lookup control and is not gated).
- Gold balance at this `n` is lopsided enough to matter: `gold_yes` is 6 of 8 at `k` = 1 and 3 of
  8 at `k` = 2, so a constant responder scores 0.75 at `k` = 1, five points under the mastery bar
  at that level, and 0.625 at `k` = 2 and `k` = 4. Wilson intervals at 8 of 8 are [0.676, 1.00]:
  an interval that contains every majority baseline in the grid. On the small-`n` binomial
  interval behaviour, the standard references are Wilson 1927 (JASA 22:209-212, DOI
  10.1080/01621459.1927.10502953, the interval this round uses) and Brown, Cai and DasGupta 2001
  (Statistical Science 16:101-133, DOI 10.1214/ss/1009213286), both verified against Crossref
  records at metadata level on 2026-11-21, neither yet in `archive/`.

So even a mastery-clearing `d_hat` at this `n` is a point estimate against a chance floor the
same instrument reports. That is not a reason to skip the calibration - it is the reason to
report `n`, interval, censoring and majority baseline together, which is what this round does and
what a future one should be required to do.

## Finding 3: this is the first calibration record for a build the nightly pool serves

Every depth sentence the loop has written is defined relative to a measured `d_hat`, and the
`N1_depth_cliff` negative branch is measurable only where the calibration arm measured
`ACC(d_hat) > 0`. Before this firing the project had calibration records only for the pinned
OLMo 2 and Pythia checkpoints, which issue #196 says the pool does not serve. So the loop had
been describing a depth axis on hardware it does not own. There is now one record on hardware it
does own - and the record's own conclusion is that the axis cannot be read at the sampler cap the
round happens to pick.

## What this moves, and what it does not

- Moves: converts "the flash slots have no depth anchor" from an absence into a measured blocker
  with a named cause (per-family sampler budget unfrozen) and a named fix.
- Does not move: no confirmatory cell, no claim, no `README.md` verdict. 8 items per level is
  below the frozen `min_n_exploratory` of 40 per cell, and the build is an
  `exploratory_only_model_class` instruction-tuned deployment, not a pin (issue #196).
- The single measurement that closes the distance: re-run this pool on the same digest with
  `max_tokens` frozen per family from a pre-gate census (2048 is the value the cross-ask family
  already runs at), report both denominators, and treat censoring above a registered share as
  `not_evaluable`. On the throughput measured here (845 s for 40 prompts at a mean of 21 s per
  record, worst record 104 s) that re-run costs about 45 minutes of a shared slot at 2048 tokens
  and cannot fit a 90-minute firing alongside outtake work: another budget consequence, not an
  excuse.
## Caveats, stated plainly

- Contention, not capability, set the wall times: mean 12 to 28 s per record by level, worst 104 s,
  with 0 transport errors. This firing's own agent is served by the same build it is probing, so
  for this pool slot the co-firing of issue #141 is structural rather than incidental.
- Trace exposure (issue #105): this deployment emits a `reasoning_content` channel on every
  record (219 to 1,627 characters). It is recorded in `message_fields` and `trace_chars` and is
  never scored. The public artifacts here carry the trace lengths, not the trace text.
- Fresh-seed policy: this pool deliberately uses the frozen `calibration_seed_base` 20260101,
  not the date-derived novel-item seed, because it is a calibration pool and not a novel band. It
  therefore says nothing about novelty or contamination.
- Weekly compute cap. By raw-record count on `main`, the nightly lane dispatched 117 prompts this
  ISO week before this firing (2026-11-16: 8 and 25; 2026-11-17: 17; 2026-11-18: 17; 2026-11-19:
  21; 2026-11-20: 29), so 157 with these 40 against the 80-a-week cap that issue #195 writes into
  `research/AGENTS.md` on the lane branch (PR #198, not `main` yet). The two Sunday firings would
  add more. Nothing anywhere carries a running counter. The loop's kill rule says a spent cap
  stops the loop and reports - it is spent, and reporting is the honest move.
